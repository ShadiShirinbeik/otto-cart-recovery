The goal

More orders from the shoppers a shop already has

Every online shop knows this moment: someone finds a product, puts it in the cart, and then closes the page. These people were one step away from buying. Most shops try to grow by paying for ads to bring in new visitors. This project looks at a cheaper idea first: win back the people who almost bought.

To do that, a shop needs answers to three questions:

  • How many? How many shoppers leave a full cart, and how many orders could we win back?
  • Who? Which of them are most likely to come back after a reminder?
  • When? What is the best moment to send that reminder?
The data

Real shopping data from OTTO

OTTO, one of Germany's biggest online shops, published this data for free (github.com/otto-de/recsys-dataset). It covers 4 weeks in August 2022 and about 12.9 million shoppers.

For every action we know four things:

  • Shopper ID an anonymous number, not a name
  • Product ID an anonymous number for each product
  • Action click, add to cart, or order
  • Time when it happened

There are no names, addresses or prices, so the data is fully anonymous and free to use.

How big is the problem?

Most full carts are left behind

This is the shopping journey of 1 million shoppers. Everyone visits, but only 29.5% put something in the cart, and only 12.6% order.

That last number is the conversion rate: out of 100 visitors, about 13 buy something.

The step this project focuses on is between the cart and the order. Of the people who filled a cart, fewer than half ended up buying.

What happens after the cart?

57% of shoppers with a cart never order

Here I zoom into the 280,924 shoppers who added something to the cart and sorted them by when they ordered.

The orange part is the problem: 159,946 people never ordered. Most buyers decide quickly, within the first hour. But a smaller group comes back hours or even days later and then buys.

The 95% confidence interval is 56.8% to 57.1%. With so many shoppers, the true rate is almost certainly in this narrow range.

Question 1 · Who should we remind?

I sorted every shopper into a type

To find the right people, I first gave every shopper one type, based on what they did:

  • Only browsed never used the cart
  • Left the cart added something, never ordered
  • Quick buyer ordered within 1 hour
  • Same-day buyer ordered 1–24 hours later
  • Slow buyer ordered more than a day later

Two small groups (grey) are left out: carts on the last two days, which may have been ordered after the data ends, and orders without a cart in the data.

Question 1 · Who should we remind?

Slow buyers take their time, and then they buy

Next I compared how a typical shopper of each type behaves. Two signals stood out:

  • Cart adds how many times they put something in the cart
  • Active days on how many different days they visited

Shoppers who left the cart and quick buyers look similar: about 2 cart adds on 2 days. Slow buyers are very different: about 7 cart adds and 8 active days. They think for a long time, but in the end they buy.

So the question became: are there cart leavers who behave like slow buyers?

Question 1 · Why this group?

More cart adds and more visits mean more orders

This heatmap shows everyone who used the cart. From left to right: how many times they added to the cart. From top to bottom: on how many different days they visited. The colour shows how many of them ordered.

The pattern is clear. With 1 cart add on 1 day, only 31% order. With 5+ cart adds on 8+ days, 74% order.

The orange boxes are my target area: 3+ cart adds and 4+ active days. Here the chance to buy is clearly higher, and the group is still big enough to matter.

Question 1 · The answer

26,919 cart leavers act like buyers

My target group are shoppers who left the cart, but added 3 or more times and came back on 4 or more days. They are 17% of all cart leavers.

In the chart every dot is one shopper. The orange dots (target group) sit in the same area as the purple dots (slow buyers who ordered later), far away from most grey dots (other cart leavers).

And among all shoppers who behave like this, 62% order, compared with 43% of all shoppers with a cart.

These people are clearly interested. They just did not finish. A small reminder could be enough.

Question 2 · When should we remind them?

Most buyers decide fast, warm shoppers take longer

Each line shows how many buyers had already ordered after a certain time since their first cart add (119,398 buyers).

Overall, half of all buyers order within 14 minutes, and 68% within one hour.

But buyers who behave like our warm shoppers (3+ cart adds, 4+ active days) are much slower: only 45% order within an hour, compared with about 80% of other buyers. The shaded area shows this gap.

So about one hour after the cart add is a good moment for a reminder: the fast deciders are done, and the warm shoppers are still thinking.

Question 2 · The best day and hour

Sunday and Monday evenings bring the most orders

Each box is one hour of one weekday. The darker the box, the more orders were placed (Berlin time).

Orders grow during the day and peak in the evening. Sunday and Monday are the busiest days, and the busiest single hour is Monday at 21:00.

The main rule stays the same: remind people about one hour after their cart, on any day. If the shop sends a second reminder to warm shoppers, Sunday or Monday evening is the best moment.

Question 2 · Through the day

Evenings are the busiest time, especially on Monday and Sunday

Each line shows the number of orders per hour on one day of the week. The shaded area is Monday.

On every day, orders rise from the morning and peak in the evening, around 20:00–21:00. Monday and Sunday evenings are clearly above the other days, and Saturday is the quietest.

Shoppers who start on the weekend also order less often overall: 10.8% vs. 13.3% on weekdays. A two-proportion z-test shows this difference is statistically significant (p < 0.001).

My recommendation

A simple plan to win back lost carts

STEP 1 · WHO

Find the warm shoppers

Cart leavers with 3+ cart adds and 4+ active days. They showed real interest but did not finish.

STEP 2 · WHEN

Wait about an hour

Most people decide on their own within an hour. Remind those who did not, on any day. A second reminder works best on Sunday or Monday evening.

STEP 3 · HOW

Send a friendly nudge

One short message that shows what is still waiting in the cart. Only to warm shoppers, so nobody gets annoyed.

The result: more orders from shoppers the shop already has, without spending more on ads.

Limits: the data has no prices, covers only one month, and the real effect of a reminder still has to be measured.

In one sentence

Remind the right people at the right time.
